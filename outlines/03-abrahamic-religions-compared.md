# Abrahamic Religions Compared — Outline

**Working title options:**
1. **Three Houses, One Story:** A Scholar's Guide to How Judaism, Christianity and Islam Read the Same Inheritance
2. **The Family Argument:** What Judaism, Christianity and Islam Share, and Where They Split
3. **Abraham's Children:** A Scholar's Guide to the Doctrines That Divide Judaism, Christianity and Islam

**Cover line (Unbound style):** WHAT YOU WERE TOLD / WHAT THE OTHER TWO SAY
**Format:** Same as Unbound, with a modified chapter pattern (below).
**Target length:** 120–150 pages.

**Thesis (one line):** Evangelical teaching often described Judaism as "the Old Testament without Jesus" and Islam as a hostile copy. Both have their own scriptures, their own ways of interpreting them, and centuries of thought that answer the same questions differently.

**Important difference from your other books:** Your readers come from evangelicalism, but this book describes two living religions from the outside. It needs:
- **Each religion described the way its own scholars and believers would recognize.** Wherever possible, use sources from inside that tradition: Jewish scholars on Judaism, Muslim scholars on Islam.
- **Sensitivity review.** A Jewish reader and a Muslim reader (ideally scholars) look over the draft before you sell it. Cupcake: I can write a careful first draft, but this book carries the most risk of a factual or tone mistake, and that check is how you catch them.
- **The variety inside each religion.** Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and Reconstructionist Judaism; Sunni, Shia and Sufi Islam; Christianity in brief. Don't present any of them as one block.

## The chapter pattern (used for every topic)

- **X.1 What You Were Told:** what evangelical teaching said about the other two religions on this topic.
- **X.2 The Shared Root:** the common text or story, where there is one.

- **X.4 Christianity:** brief, with the range across traditions.
- **X.5 Islam:** texts (Qur'an, hadith), how they're interpreted, and the range of views.

## Before You Begin

- A note on method: describe each religion in the terms its own people would use; no religion is graded.
- Why an exvangelical needs this book: the "other religions" unit in youth group, and what it left out.
- A short history: ancient Israel and Second Temple Judaism; the early church's split from the synagogue; rabbinic Judaism after 70 CE; the rise of Islam in the 7th century; Christian-Jewish-Muslim history in brief, including the hard parts (antisemitism, the Crusades, colonialism).
- Words to know: Tanakh, Torah, Talmud, halakha, Qur'an, hadith, sharia, tafsir, ummah.

## PART ONE · GOD AND REVELATION

1. **One God, Three Ways.** The Shema, the Trinity and *tawhid*. Why Judaism and Islam see the Trinity as a problem, and how Christians answer that.
2. **Scripture.** Written Torah and Oral Torah; the Bible and its canons; the Qur'an as the direct word of God and the role of hadith. How each religion thinks about the scriptures of the others.
3. **Prophets.** Moses, the Hebrew prophets, Jesus and Muhammad. Prophecy ending, continuing, or completed. The "seal of the prophets."
4. **Law and Grace.** Halakha, the Christian "freedom from the Law," and sharia as a whole way of life. Why "legalism" is a bad translation of what Jewish and Islamic law are for.

## PART TWO · THE SHARED STORIES

5. **Adam, Eve and Sin.** Christian original sin vs Jewish *yetzer hara* (the inclination to do wrong) vs the Islamic account of Adam's repentance and forgiveness. Neither Judaism nor Islam teaches inherited guilt.
6. **Abraham and the Sacrifice.** The *Akedah* (Isaac) and the Qur'anic account, where the son is unnamed and Muslim tradition mostly identifies him as Ishmael. Eid al-Adha and Rosh Hashanah readings.
7. **Satan.** *Ha-satan* as accuser in the Hebrew Bible, the Christian devil, and Iblis in the Qur'an.
8. **Jesus.** Judaism: a Jewish teacher, not the Messiah. Christianity: divine Son. Islam: a prophet born of a virgin, the Messiah (*al-Masih*), not divine. The crucifixion question (Qur'an 4:157) and the range of Muslim interpretations of it.
9. **Mary.** Her place in each religion, including the Qur'anic chapter named after her (Surah Maryam).

## PART THREE · SALVATION, DEATH AND THE END

10. **What Saves?** Covenant and *teshuvah* (repentance), grace through Christ, and faith plus deeds under God's mercy.
11. **Afterlife.** Sheol, *Olam Ha-Ba* and Gehinnom; heaven and hell; *Jannah* and *Jahannam*. How much each religion actually specifies.
12. **The Messiah and the End.** The Jewish messianic age, the Christian Second Coming, and Islamic eschatology (the Mahdi and Jesus's return in Islamic tradition).
13. **Who Gets In?** Exclusivism and inclusivism in each religion. Rabbinic teaching on "the righteous of the nations"; Qur'an 2:62 and how interpreters read it.

## PART FOUR · LIFE

14. **Prayer, Sabbath and the Week.** Shabbat, Sunday, Friday *jumu'ah*. Daily prayer in each religion.
15. **Food and Purity.** Kashrut, Christian freedom from food laws (Acts 10–15, linking back to Unbound), and halal.
16. **Gender, Marriage and Family.** The range of views in each religion, including current debates.
17. **Sexuality.** The texts in each religion and the range of present-day positions. Short, sourced.
18. **Violence and Peace.** The hard texts in all three scriptures, and how each religion's own interpreters handle them. Same standard for all three.
