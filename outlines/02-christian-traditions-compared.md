# Christian Traditions Compared — Outline

**Working title options:**
1. **Never Settled:** A Scholar's Guide to the Doctrines Christians Have Always Disagreed On
2. **Which Christianity?** What Your Church Taught as "What Christians Believe," and What the Rest of Christianity Says
3. **The Other Answers:** A Scholar's Guide to How Christian Traditions Disagree

**Cover line (Unbound style):** WHAT YOU WERE TOLD / WHAT THE REST OF THE CHURCH SAYS
**Format:** Same as Unbound: Before You Begin, three parts, numbered sections, the strongest case on each side, conversation guides, appendices and workbook pages.
**Target length:** 140–170 pages. This is the biggest of the three, because there are so many doctrines.

**Thesis (one line):** What many evangelical churches taught as "the Christian position" is usually one position among several. Each of the others has a long history, serious scholars and millions of believers behind it.

---

## The chapter pattern (used for every doctrine)

- **X.1 What You Were Told:** the typical evangelical version, stated fairly.
- **X.2 The Texts Everyone Cites:** the key verses, quoted.
- **X.3 Where the Idea Came From:** history, dates, councils and key figures.
- **X.4 The Positions, Side by Side:** a comparison table (Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican/Episcopal, Methodist/Wesleyan, Baptist, Pentecostal, nondenominational evangelical, plus others where relevant).
- **X.5 The Key Word:** one Greek, Hebrew or Latin term that the disagreement turns on.
- **X.6 The Strongest Case for Each Position**
- **X.7 What the Texts Can and Cannot Settle**
- **X.8 If This Comes Up in Conversation**
- **Write in This Book:** reflection prompts.

## Before You Begin

- A note on method: description, not ranking, and no tradition is the villain.
- What "tradition" means here: denominations vs families of churches. How the book picks which ones go in the table.
- A short map of Christian history: the early church, the East-West split (1054), the Reformation (1517 onward), the Radical Reformation, the Wesleyan revivals, American revivalism, Pentecostalism (1900s), modern evangelicalism and fundamentalism.
- Where the groups that call themselves Christian but sit outside historic creeds fit in (Latter-day Saints, Jehovah's Witnesses, Oneness Pentecostals). They are included as their own column with a clear note, and the book doesn't rule on who counts as a "real" Christian.

## PART ONE · AUTHORITY: WHO GETS TO DECIDE

1. **Which Bible? The Canon Question.** Protestant 66 books, the Catholic 73, Orthodox canons, the Ethiopian Orthodox canon. The Apocrypha and deuterocanon, and when each canon was fixed.
2. **Inerrancy and How to Read Scripture.** The Chicago Statement (1978) vs Catholic, Orthodox and mainline views of inspiration. Literal vs historical-critical reading.
3. **Scripture, Tradition and Church Authority.** *Sola scriptura*, papal authority, the ecumenical councils, the Wesleyan quadrilateral, congregational autonomy.
4. **Creeds and the Trinity.** Nicaea (325), Chalcedon (451), the *filioque* dispute, and non-trinitarian groups.

## PART TWO · SALVATION: WHAT'S AT STAKE AND WHO'S IN

5. **Original Sin.** Augustine's inherited guilt vs the Eastern Orthodox idea of ancestral sin. The "age of accountability."
6. **How Are People Saved?** Faith alone, faith and works, grace through the sacraments, and *theosis* (becoming like God).
7. **Predestination and Free Will.** Calvinism vs Arminianism, the Synod of Dort (1618–19), and Catholic and Orthodox positions.
8. **Can You Lose It?** Eternal security, perseverance, falling away.
9. **The Atonement.** Penal substitution, satisfaction (Anselm), Christus Victor, moral influence, and recapitulation. When penal substitution became dominant.
10. **Hell.** Eternal conscious torment, annihilationism (conditional immortality), Christian universalism, and purgatory. The words behind "hell": *Sheol*, *Hades*, *Gehenna*, *Tartarus*.
11. **Who Else Gets Saved?** Exclusivism, inclusivism, pluralism, and the Catholic teaching after Vatican II.

## PART THREE · PRACTICE: SACRAMENTS, SPIRIT, AND THE END

12. **Baptism.** Infant vs believer's baptism, sprinkling vs immersion, and whether baptism saves (baptismal regeneration) or symbolizes.
13. **Communion.** Transubstantiation, Orthodox mystery, Lutheran sacramental union, Reformed spiritual presence, and the memorial view.
14. **The Holy Spirit and the Gifts.** Cessationism vs continuationism, tongues, Azusa Street (1906), and the charismatic renewal.
15. **Mary and the Saints.** Intercession, the Marian dogmas, icons and the Protestant objections.
16. **The Sabbath and Worship.** Saturday vs Sunday, liturgical vs free-church worship, and the church calendar.
17. **The End Times.** Pre-, post- and amillennialism, preterism, and the pretribulation Rapture (Darby, 1830s; spread by the Scofield Reference Bible, 1909). How *Left Behind* shaped a generation.

## PART FOUR · BODIES AND HOMES: GENDER, SEX AND MARRIAGE

18. **Women in Ministry.** Complementarian vs egalitarian views, and when different denominations began ordaining women.
19. **Marriage and Divorce.** Sacrament vs covenant, annulment, remarriage, Orthodox "economy."
20. **Sex, Contraception and Celibacy.** *Humanae Vitae* (1968), Protestant acceptance of contraception, clergy celibacy East and West.
21. **LGBTQ+ Inclusion.** Where each tradition stands now, and the splits (e.g. the United Methodist separation). Short, pointing to Unbound for the texts.
22. **Abortion.** How positions changed, including the evangelical shift in the late 1970s. *Cupcake: needs careful sourcing; Randall Balmer's work is a likely starting point.*

## PART FIVE · CHURCH AND WORLD

23. **Creation and Evolution.** Young-earth creationism (a mostly 20th-century revival, after *The Genesis Flood*, 1961), old-earth views, theistic evolution, Catholic positions.
24. **Church and State.** Two kingdoms, Anabaptist separation, Catholic social teaching, Christian nationalism.
25. **Money.** Tithing, the prosperity gospel, and voluntary poverty.

## Overlap with *What They Actually Taught You*

That guide already covers 10 evangelical doctrines. This book compares *traditions*, but hell, the Rapture and inerrancy will overlap with it. Choices:
- (a) Keep the overlap short and point readers to the other guide, or
- (b) Treat this as the bigger book and later fold the doctrine guide into it.
