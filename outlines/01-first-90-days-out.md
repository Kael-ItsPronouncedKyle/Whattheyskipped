# First 90 Days Out — Outline (rewrite from scratch)

**Working title:** First 90 Days Out
**Subtitle options:**
1. A Workbook for the Space Between
2. A Field Guide for Leaving Evangelical Faith Without Losing Yourself
3. What to Do With the First Three Months After the Floor Moves

**Cover line (Unbound style):** WHAT YOU WERE TOLD / WHAT HAPPENS NEXT
**Format:** Workbook. Same house style as Unbound: numbered sections, "Write in this book" pages, sources at the end of each chapter, appendices.
**Target length:** 90–110 pages.

**How it differs from the current $19 edition:** Unbound is a scholarship book. This one is a practical one, but it keeps Unbound's rules. Every factual claim is sourced, it never assumes how the reader's story ends, and it never shames the reader for staying, leaving or landing somewhere in between.

---

## Before You Begin

- **A note on where you are.** You may have left on purpose, been pushed out, or still be sitting in a pew. All three are welcome here.
- **What this workbook is not.** It isn't therapy and it isn't a deconversion manual. It isn't anti-Christian, and it doesn't push you toward any destination, whether faith, no faith or something else.
- **If it's midnight and this is bad.** Crisis resources go on page 1, not at the back (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the US, Trevor Project for LGBTQ+ youth, and international equivalents in Appendix A).
- **How to use the 90 days.** Three phases of 30 days, one short page a day, and you can skip days without failing. There are three ways through: daily, weekly, or crisis-only.
- **A note on who wrote this.** Founder voice: why this exists.

---

## PART ONE · DAYS 1–30 · STEADY THE GROUND

The goal of Part One is safety and orientation. Nothing gets decided yet.

1. **What Just Happened.** Naming the experience: deconstruction, deconversion, faith transition and "church hurt." What the words mean, where they came from, and why the label matters less than the person using it.
2. **The Body Keeps Score Here Too.** Fear responses after high-control religion: hell anxiety, intrusive "what if I'm wrong" loops, sleep and panic. What the research does and does not say about "religious trauma." *(Source the term carefully: Marlene Winell's "Religious Trauma Syndrome" concept, and current peer-reviewed work. Say clearly that it is not a formal DSM diagnosis.)*
3. **Practical Safety.** Housing, money, phone plans, shared accounts, being a minor or an adult child under a family roof, being LGBTQ+ in an unsafe home. A checklist chapter.
4. **The Hell Question, Day One Version.** A short first-aid chapter for fear of hell. It points to the full treatment in *What They Actually Taught You* and the new comparison book, so it upsells naturally.
5. **Who to Tell, and When.** Telling no one, telling one person, or telling everyone. A worksheet that ranks the people in your life by safety rather than by closeness.

**Workbook pages, days 1–30:** One prompt each. Grounding exercises, a fear log, a "what I actually believe today" snapshot (dated, so the reader can compare on Day 90).

---

## PART TWO · DAYS 31–60 · LOOK AT WHAT YOU WERE GIVEN

The goal of Part Two is to examine and grieve. The reader can now look at things that would have been too much on Day 3.

6. **Sorting the Inheritance.** What you were taught falls into three piles: true, harmful, and unexamined. A sorting worksheet that keeps the good (music, community, charity, moral seriousness) without having to keep everything.
7. **Grief Without a Funeral.** Losing community, worship, certainty, a future you'd planned, and sometimes a marriage or family closeness. Grief models used honestly, without claiming stages are a fixed sequence.
8. **Purity Culture and Your Body.** Shame, sex, gender roles and modesty teaching. What the research says about purity-culture effects (source it, e.g. Linda Kay Klein's *Pure*, 2018, and peer-reviewed studies).
9. **Family, Marriage and Mixed-Faith Homes.** Spouses who stay, parents who grieve, kids' questions, holidays. Scripts for each, plus boundaries that don't require a fight.
10. **Your Brain on Black-and-White Thinking.** Why everything can feel either totally true or totally false right now, and why that's the old system's habit, not the conclusion you've reached.

**Workbook pages, days 31–60:** Letters you won't send, a values inventory, a "rules I still follow without knowing why" list, and holiday planning pages.

---

## PART THREE · DAYS 61–90 · BUILD SOMETHING ON PURPOSE

The goal of Part Three is to begin rebuilding, at your own pace, toward whatever you choose.

11. **Ethics Without the Old Rulebook.** How people build moral lives inside and outside religion. A survey of options, not a sermon.
12. **Finding Your People.** Community after church: secular groups, affirming congregations, online spaces, and why it takes longer than it should.
13. **Choosing Help.** How to find a therapist who won't re-traumatize you or try to win you back. Questions to ask a therapist. Faith-based vs secular counseling, and what licensure means.
14. **Where People Land.** Progressive Christianity, other traditions, spiritual-but-not-religious, agnosticism, atheism. Short, fair portraits of each. No ranking.
15. **Day 90 and After.** Revisit the Day 1 snapshot. What changed, what didn't, and what the next 90 days could look like.

**Workbook pages, days 61–90:** A "new rules I chose" page, a community map, a therapy-search worksheet, and a letter to your Day 1 self.
