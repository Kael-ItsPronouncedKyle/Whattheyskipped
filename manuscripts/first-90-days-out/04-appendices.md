## B · GLOSSARY

**Affirming.** A church or group that fully includes LGBTQ+ people, including in leadership and marriage.

**Agnostic.** Someone who thinks the existence of God can't be known, or who hasn't decided.

**Ambiguous loss.** Pauline Boss's term for a loss without clear resolution (Chapter 7).

**Apologetics.** Arguments in defense of a religious belief.

**Biblical counseling.** Counseling based on the view that Scripture alone is sufficient for emotional problems; often practiced by people without state licenses (Chapter 13).

**Complementarian / egalitarian.** Two views of gender roles. Complementarians hold that men and women have different God-given roles, with men leading in church and home. Egalitarians hold that roles shouldn't be assigned by gender.

**Conversion therapy.** Attempts to change a person's sexual orientation or gender identity. Opposed by major US medical and mental health associations.

**Deconstruction.** Taking apart inherited beliefs to examine them (Chapter 1).

**Deconversion.** Leaving a religion entirely.

**Exvangelical.** A former evangelical.

**High-control group.** Any group, religious or not, that tightly controls members' information, relationships, behavior, or thinking.

**Humanism.** A worldview grounding ethics and meaning in human reason and compassion rather than the supernatural.

**Mainline Protestant.** Older, generally more progressive Protestant denominations in the US, such as the Episcopal Church, the ELCA, the PC(USA), and the United Church of Christ.

**Nones.** People who don't identify with any religion. A survey category, not a belief system.

**Purity culture.** The evangelical movement emphasizing sexual abstinence before marriage, especially in the 1990s and 2000s (Chapter 8).

**Religious trauma.** Lasting harm from religious beliefs, practices, or communities. Not a formal diagnosis (Chapter 2).

**Sliding scale.** Fees adjusted to income.

## C · SCRIPTS

Short openers for hard conversations. Change the words to sound like you.

**To a safe friend, for the first time**
> "I've been going through some stuff with my faith. I'm not looking for answers. I just didn't want to carry it alone."

**To a parent who's scared**
> "I know you're worried about me. I'm okay. I'd like us to keep talking even though we see this differently."

**To a parent who keeps pushing**
> "I'm happy to tell you where I am. I'm not going to debate it. If it turns into a debate, I'm going to head out and we can try another day."

**To a spouse who still believes**
> "I'm not trying to take your faith from you. I need room to be honest about mine. Can we figure out what that looks like together?"

**To a pastor who calls**
> "Thank you for checking on me. I'm taking some time away, and I'm not ready to talk about it. I'll reach out if that changes."

**To a coworker who invites you to church**
> "Thanks for thinking of me. Church isn't something I'm doing right now, but I'd be up for lunch sometime."

**To your kid**
> "Mom and I believe different things about God right now. That's okay. You can ask either of us anything."

**When you need to leave a conversation**
> "I can tell this is hard for both of us. I'm going to stop here for today."

## D · FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Is this book trying to get me to stop believing in God?**
No. It's trying to get you through the first three months in good shape. Where you land is up to you.

**I'm still going to church. Is this book for me?**
Yes. Many people work through these questions while still attending. Skip anything that doesn't fit.

**What if I go back?**
Then you go back. People return to faith, sometimes the same faith and sometimes a different version. Coming back on purpose, after looking hard at it, is different from never having looked.

**I'm angry all the time. Is something wrong with me?**
Anger is a common and often appropriate response to being misled or hurt. If it's taking over your days, or you're scaring yourself or others, that's a good reason to find a therapist.

**Why are there sources in a workbook?**
Because the people who wrote it believe you deserve to check claims for yourself. You may have spent years being asked to trust confident people. This book doesn't ask that.

**What if someone finds this book?**
That's a real concern for some readers. See Chapter 3 on privacy.

**Can I share this with a friend?**
Yes, with one person at a time. Please don't post or redistribute it in bulk.

## G · THE GROUP TRACK

This workbook can be used with a friend or a small group. Some suggestions:

- **Meet every two weeks** for about ninety minutes. Six meetings cover the book.
- **Session plan:** Chapters 1–3; Chapters 4–5; Chapters 6–7; Chapters 8–10; Chapters 11–13; Chapters 14–15.
- **Ground rules worth saying out loud at the first meeting:**
  - What's said in the group stays in the group.
  - Nobody has to share their workbook pages.
  - No one is trying to change anyone's mind, in any direction.
  - "I don't know" is a complete answer.
- **A good opening question for every meeting:** "What's one thing from the last two weeks that was harder, and one that was easier?"
- **If someone is in crisis,** pause the group and point them to Appendix A. A peer group is not a substitute for professional help.

## H · CHECKING YOUR WORK

You don't have to take this book's word for anything. Here's how to check a claim:

1. **Find the source.** Every chapter ends with its sources. Look up the book or article.
2. **Check the date.** Research and laws change. A 2011 article and a 2023 survey may not agree, and later doesn't always mean better, but it matters.
3. **Ask who paid for it.** This book notes funding where it's relevant (as with the 2023 religious trauma survey).
4. **Look for disagreement.** If only one source says something, treat it as a claim, not a fact.
5. **Notice how a claim makes you feel.** If something feels too good or too terrible to be true, slow down. That's when everyone, including the author of this book, is most likely to be fooled.

If you find an error, write to hello@whattheyskipped.com. Corrections will be made in the next printing.
