# Sensitivity Read: Trans Man Reader

**Reader perspective:** trans man, early 20s, raised in a nondenominational megachurch youth group, came out recently, on a parent's health insurance, still living at home or just moved out.

**Manuscript:** *First 90 Days Out*, files 01–04, read in order.

---

## Overall verdict

I trusted this book from the first page. The crisis page lists Trans Lifeline and says plainly which lines can involve emergency services. It doesn't try to talk me into or out of God. And "the one the handouts were about" made me stop reading for a minute. But after the author's note, the book stops seeing me. It was written by a trans man, and the reader it actually pictures is a cis woman raised in purity culture, a married person with kids, or a gay kid whose secret is still a secret. The places where trans readers differ, it either leaves out or gets slightly wrong. It doesn't mention that my parents' insurance mails them a statement every time I fill a testosterone prescription. It doesn't say that I got the *girls'* version of purity culture and am now supposed to read the "men" sentence as mine. It doesn't account for my family's "disagreement" being about whether to use my name. And its conversion-therapy warning names the rebrands aimed at gay people but not the one aimed at me. Nothing here would hurt me badly. Several things would make me close the book and feel that "trans" on the author page was a credential, not an invitation. Six fixes are needed before print, and all of them are additions; none requires rewriting the book's voice. Where I suggest new factual claims, they are marked [VERIFY] so the book's sourcing standard holds.

---

## Notes

### 1. Crisis page: needs a fallback for "press 3" and an honest note on Trans Lifeline's hours
- **Location:** "If You Need Help Right Now," and Appendix A table. Quote: *"LGBTQ+ people under 25 can call 988 and **press 3**"* and *"Trans Lifeline… Weekday hours only"*
- **Reaction:** Everybody in my group chats heard in 2025 that the press-3 line was shut down. Your source note says it came back, but the page I'd actually read at 2 a.m. doesn't say so, and I'd doubt it. If I press 3 and it doesn't work, I don't have the energy left to try a second number. The trans-run line is the one I'd call first, and at 2 a.m., when Chapter 2 says the fear hits hardest, it's closed.
- **Suggested change:** After the 988 bullet, add: *"(This press-3 option was paused in 2025 and restored in 2026. If it isn't offered when you call, hang up and call or text The Trevor Project, below. It answers 24/7.)"* [VERIFY Trevor's 24/7 status on the day of layout]. After the Trans Lifeline bullet, add: *"If it's outside their hours, Trevor (under 25) and Crisis Text Line are open around the clock. You can tell any of them you're trans before you say anything else."* Do the same in Appendix A.
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 2. KEEP: the crisis page is written by someone who knows why trans people hesitate to call
- **Location:** Crisis page. Quote: *"988 can, in rare cases of immediate danger, involve emergency services"* / *"It does not call emergency services without your consent."*
- **Reaction:** This is the line that told me the author is one of us. Fear of police showing up is the number one reason trans people I know don't call, and most crisis pages hide it. Telling me straight and giving me a peer-run option is respect.
- **Suggested change:** None. Keep it word for word, and keep the page first.
- **Severity:** KEEP

### 3. Parent's insurance: the book flags it and then doesn't tell me the thing I need to know
- **Location:** Ch 3.1 Dependency Checklist. Quote: *"Health insurance. Is it through a religious employer or a parent's plan?"* Also Ch 13.4: *"If you have insurance, your provider directory lists in-network therapists."*
- **Reaction:** This is the most practical danger in my life right now, and the book stops at a yes/no question. When I bill an endocrinologist, a therapist, or a T prescription to Dad's plan, an Explanation of Benefits goes to *him*. Mom may also have proxy access to my patient portal from when I was 16. Chapter 13 sends me straight into that trap.
- **Suggested change:** Add a box after 3.1, *"If you're on a parent's health plan"*: *"Under US law you can usually stay on a parent's plan until 26. But the plan normally mails statements (Explanations of Benefits) to the policyholder, and those can list the doctor, the service, and sometimes the prescription. Under HIPAA you can ask the insurer, in writing, to send your statements to a different address or to your own online account, and a plan must agree if you say that disclosure could endanger you. Some states go further. Also check: whether a parent still has proxy access to your patient portal from when you were a minor; whose phone number and email are on file with your doctor and pharmacy; and whether the pharmacy app is logged in on a family device."* Cross-reference it from 13.4. Source: 45 C.F.R. §164.522(b)(1)(ii); ACA dependent coverage to age 26, 42 U.S.C. §300gg-14 [VERIFY both, plus a state confidentiality example such as California Civil Code §56.107].
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 4. Dependency checklist: leaves out the documents I'd need if I got kicked out
- **Location:** Ch 3.1. The list covers housing, income, insurance, school, phone, car, and childcare, and no documents.
- **Reaction:** If I have to leave tonight, I'm going without my birth certificate, Social Security card, and passport, because Mom keeps them in the fire safe. I need those to get a job, a lease, and a legal name change. For a trans kid at risk of being kicked out, this matters more than whose name the car is in.
- **Suggested change:** Add a checklist bullet: *"**Documents.** Who holds your birth certificate, Social Security card, passport, and your car title? Could you get them, or copies, without asking? If you're planning a legal name or gender-marker change, these are the papers you'll need."* Add to Appendix A: *"Advocates for Trans Equality (formerly the National Center for Transgender Equality) keeps state-by-state guides to changing your name and ID."* [VERIFY current org name and URL]. Add Day 18: *"…and what papers would you need to take with you?"*
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 5. Purity culture chapter: I got the girls' version, and the book doesn't know what to do with me
- **Location:** Ch 8.1–8.2. Quotes: *"modesty teaching that held girls responsible for boys' thoughts"*; *"Purity culture didn't only affect women. Men describe being taught that they were one glance away from lust… LGBTQ+ people were often taught that their bodies were a problem with no solution."*
- **Reaction:** I sat through the girls' purity talks. The "don't be a stumbling block" lectures. Being told to write letters to my future husband. The shirt check before youth camp, focused on exactly the part of my body I already couldn't stand. The chapter files its readers as women, men, or LGBTQ+, and I'm all three at an angle: women's teaching aimed at me, while I'm now in the group it called predators. Being told that men are "one glance away from lust" and then *becoming a man* is its own particular mess, and nobody has named it for me. "A problem with no solution" is also wrong. They gave us a solution: submit to your body, because "God doesn't make mistakes."
- **Suggested change:** Add a subsection 8.3, **"If you were taught the other half's rules."** Suggested text: *"Some readers were raised under purity teaching aimed at girls and aren't women: trans men and other transmasculine people, and some nonbinary people. If that's you, you may carry two sets of messages that don't fit together. One is the modesty rules about a body you didn't recognize as yours. The other is the warning that men are dangerous, which can turn into a quiet fear that transitioning means becoming the danger. Neither was ever a fact about you. You were also probably given a 'solution': that your body was God's decision and your discomfort was rebellion. 'God doesn't make mistakes' and Genesis 1:27 ('male and female he created them') were often used this way. Formal statements made it explicit. The 2017 Nashville Statement, from the Council on Biblical Manhood and Womanhood, says adopting a transgender self-conception is inconsistent with God's purposes (Article 7). Knowing it was a published position, not a law of nature, can help."* Change the last sentence of 8.2 to *"…LGBTQ+ people were often taught that their bodies or desires were a problem, and that the only acceptable solution was to submit to the rules."* Add sources: Nashville Statement (CBMW, 2017), Articles 7 and 10 [VERIFY wording].
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 6. Purity chapter "What helps": add a line about my chest, not only pelvic floor PT
- **Location:** Ch 8.3. Quote: *"If you're experiencing sexual pain or anxiety… Pelvic floor physical therapy is a real specialty."*
- **Reaction:** The body help in this chapter is written for married cis women. My body issue is that I bind eight hours a day, and nobody at church ever told me anything about my body except to cover it.
- **Suggested change:** Add a bullet: *"**If you bind,** use a binder made for it, never tape or elastic bandages; take breaks, and don't sleep or exercise hard in it. Tell a doctor if you have pain or trouble breathing. You're allowed to ask a clinic if they're trans-competent before you book."* Source: Peitzmeier et al., "Health impact of chest binding among transgender adults," *Culture, Health & Sexuality* 19, no. 1 (2017) [VERIFY]. Also add to the "ask a provider" sentence: *"…experience with clients from conservative religious backgrounds, or with trans clients."*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 7. Family chapter treats misgendering like a theology disagreement
- **Location:** Ch 9.2 table and scripts. Quotes: *"Often it's fear wearing the costume of anger"*; *"I'd like us to keep having dinner together even though we disagree."*
- **Reaction:** The table reads my parents generously, and that's fair. But it hands me a script that frames everything as "disagreement," and in my house the disagreement is about whether to call me by my name. Dinner where I'm "she" all night isn't a peaceful difference of opinion. The book's own "both" logic could talk me into putting up with it. My parents also tell one story about me: their daughter rebelled against God, and being trans is the proof, or the cause. The book never mentions that they'll braid the two together.
- **Suggested change:** Add rows to the 9.2 table:
  | They say | They often mean |
  |---|---|
  | "You'll always be our daughter." | "I'm grieving someone I thought I knew." (Grief is real; it's still not a reason to be misnamed.) |
  | "This is the internet / your friends." | "I can't believe you got here on your own." |
  | "You walked away from God and now this." | "I need one explanation for two things I'm scared of." |

  Add a paragraph: *"If you're trans, your family may treat your faith and your gender as one story: a rebellious daughter, an angry son, a phase that started with doubt. They're separate, and you get to talk about them separately. Disagreeing about God is something two people can live with across a dinner table. Being called by the wrong name or pronoun every night is not a difference of opinion; it's something being done to you. You can make that a boundary, the same way you'd refuse a debate."* Add a script: *"You don't have to agree with me about God. I need you to use my name. If you can't tonight, I'm going to head out, and we'll try again."* Mirror it in Appendix C.
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 8. Conversion therapy: the rebrand aimed at trans people is missing, and the therapist questions are LGB-only
- **Location:** Ch 13.3 and 13.5. Quotes: *"'Have you worked with LGBTQ+ clients?' (if relevant)"*; *"rebrands, such as 'reintegrative therapy' or counseling for 'unwanted same-sex attraction.'"*
- **Reaction:** The chapter names the rebrands aimed at gay people and not the one aimed at me. If my parents offer to pay for a "Christian therapist who'll help you explore," that's the trap I'm walking into. "Have you worked with LGBTQ+ clients" is a box any therapist can tick after seeing one lesbian couple.
- **Suggested change:** In 13.5, after the rebrands list, add: *"Efforts aimed at trans people are often presented as neutral 'exploration,' for example 'gender exploratory therapy,' which critics describe as gender identity change efforts under another name. Be especially careful if a family member chooses and pays for the therapist."* [VERIFY with APA 2021 Resolution on Gender Identity Change Efforts, already cited]. In 13.3, replace the LGBTQ+ bullet with two: *"'Have you worked with LGBTQ+ clients? With trans clients specifically?'"* and *"If relevant: 'Do you write letters for gender-affirming care, and do you require a set number of sessions first?'"* Add to 13.4: *"If a parent's insurance pays, see the box in Chapter 3 before you book."*
- **Severity:** MUST FIX

### 9. KEEP: "Count down, if you can… It's planning."

- **Reaction:** I've hated myself for being "fake" at home. Reading that waiting is planning, not cowardice, actually helped. Keep it exactly.

### 10. "If you're living with family" assumes faith is my only secret
- **Location:** Ch 3.2. Quote: *"If you're LGBTQ+ and worried about being kicked out, the Trevor Project… Many cities have organizations…"*
- **Reaction:** For me the big conversation already happened, and it was about gender, not God. My voice is dropping on T, so I couldn't keep it secret if I tried. My parents expect me back at church *because* I'm trans, so that I can be fixed. "Many cities have organizations" is too vague for the person this section calls "the hardest spot in this book."
- **Suggested change:** Add: *"**If you've already come out about something else,** being trans or gay, your parents may be pushing church harder now, as a fix. You can still keep your faith questions private while being open about who you are. Those are different conversations, and you can have them in whatever order keeps you safest."* Replace "Many cities have organizations" with *"Your nearest LGBTQ+ community center (CenterLink keeps a directory at lgbtcenters.org) can point you to local youth housing and emergency help."* [VERIFY URL].
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 11. Privacy section is one-dimensional for a trans reader
- **Location:** Ch 3.3. Quote: *"use a private browsing window for research"*
- **Reaction:** Private browsing won't help when my binder arrives in a box addressed to my deadname, or when Mom gets the family-plan pharmacy text, or when Life360 shows me at the Planned Parenthood. My search history isn't the risk. The physical world is.
- **Suggested change:** Add bullets: *"Check family-sharing settings (Screen Time, Google Family Link, location-sharing apps) on any device a parent set up."*; *"Calls to some numbers can show up on an itemized family phone bill. Check before you call from a shared plan."* [VERIFY whether 988/Trevor/Crisis Text Line appear on major carriers' bills]; *"Things you order online, and pharmacy notifications, can reach the house. A friend's address, a locker, or in-store pickup can help."*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 12. The author's identity shows up once, then disappears
- **Location:** "A Note on Who Wrote This." Quote: *"written by a trans man… the one passing out the handouts, and later the one the handouts were about."*
- **Reaction:** That line is the best sentence in the front matter for me. It's also the last time the book acknowledges a trans person exists, other than one hotline and one line in the conversion-therapy section. I don't need the author's life story, but I noticed the silence, and it made me wonder if he was told to keep it down.
- **Suggested change:** Keep the line. Add one sentence after "…the handouts were about": *"Some of what's in this book, especially about families, bodies, and finding help, he learned the hard way as a trans person leaving a church that had a position on him. He's written it so it's useful whether or not that's your story."* Then let the trans-specific additions in Notes 3–8 do the rest, without making it a memoir.
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 13. KEEP: "rules about gender that never fit you"
- **Location:** Ch 6.2, Return pile. Quote: *"rules about gender that never fit you"*
- **Reaction:** This was the one line in Part Two where I felt the book saw me without making a fuss about it. It's also true for cis readers, which is why it works.
- **Suggested change:** Keep. Optionally add to the Return examples: *"the idea that being a man means leading, protecting, and never needing help"* (see Note 14).
- **Severity:** KEEP

### 14. Masculinity: the only model of manhood I was given was the church's
- **Location:** Nowhere in the body. Glossary only: *"Complementarian… men leading in church and home."*
- **Reaction:** I'm building a life as a man, and the only blueprint I was handed was the men's ministry one: headship, provider, *Wild at Heart*, "real men lead their homes." Nobody ever pitched it to *me*, but it's the template in my head, and so is its opposite: men are dangerous. I need permission to put "biblical manhood" in a pile and sort it like anything else. Cis guys leaving church need that too.
- **Suggested change:** Add a short 6.4, **"The gender scripts."** *"Most evangelical churches teach a specific model of what men and women are for, often under the name 'biblical manhood and womanhood' (the complementarian view, see Glossary). Men's ministries, women's retreats, and gender-split youth small groups taught it by practice even when nobody said it out loud. Whatever your gender, and especially if you're figuring out what kind of man or woman or person you want to be, these scripts belong in the sorting piles like everything else. Some readers keep pieces (showing up, keeping promises). Many return others (that leadership belongs to one gender, that needing help is weakness)."* Source: CBMW, *Danvers Statement* (1987) [VERIFY]. Add a Day 34 variant: *"What were you taught a man or a woman is for? Which parts would you keep?"*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 15. Workbook prompts that land on dysphoria without warning
- **Location:** Day 3 (*"what you wore"*), Day 41 (*"What future did you imagine for yourself at sixteen?"*), Day 45 (*"What were you taught about your body before you turned twelve?"*), Ch 7.1 (*"the church wedding, raising kids in the same youth group"*).
- **Reaction:** Day 3 is me in a dress I was made to wear, and Day 45 is the training-bra talk. Day 41's "future I imagined" was a future someone else imagined *for* me: bride, wife, mother. I can do these prompts, but without a word of warning they're an ambush, and on a bad week they'd make me quit the book.
- **Suggested change:** Add one line to the Part Two workbook intro (or before Week 7): *"Some of these prompts ask about your body or your childhood self. If you were raised as a gender that isn't yours, you can answer about what you were* taught *girls or boys were, as a teaching rather than a description of you, or skip the prompt."* Change Day 41 to *"What future did you imagine for yourself at sixteen, or what future did other people imagine for you? What's left of it?"*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 16. "Both" thinking needs one sentence of protection
- **Location:** Ch 10.1 and 10.4. Quote: *"Their parents used to be heroes and now they're villains."*
- **Reaction:** I agree with the chapter, and I can feel how it'll be used against me: *you're being black-and-white about your parents.* My dad calling transition "mutilation" isn't something I owe a "both" sentence to.
- **Suggested change:** Add at the end of 10.4: *"Seeing complexity doesn't mean calling harm neutral. 'My dad loves me, and the way he talks about who I am hurts me' is a both sentence. So is 'I can understand why they're scared, and I still need distance.' Both-thinking is a tool for you, not a reason to accept treatment you've already decided is not okay."*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX

### 17. Community: "affirming" often means LGB-affirming, and church men's groups are a new minefield
- **Location:** Ch 12.2. Quote: *"many denominations and individual churches now welcome doubters and LGBTQ+ people. Ask directly about their stance"*
- **Reaction:** I've been to an "affirming" church that had a rainbow flag and then asked which women's group I'd like to join. If I keep some faith, my real question is whether I'd be treated as a man there, men's group included, or be the trans exception. Also, the list doesn't include trans-specific peer groups, which is where I've actually found my people.
- **Suggested change:** Change to: *"Ask directly about their stance, and if you're trans, ask specifically about trans people: whether they'll use your name in the directory, which groups you'd be welcome in, and whether any trans people are already members. 'Affirming' sometimes means gay-affirming only."* Add a bullet: *"**Trans and queer peer groups.** Many LGBTQ+ centers run groups for trans adults, including ones for people from religious backgrounds."* Update the Glossary "Affirming" entry: *"…including in leadership, marriage, and, in fully affirming spaces, recognizing trans members' genders."*
- **Severity:** SHOULD FIX
